Bolo on desktop

How Bolo should work in a desktop browser, and why it should not be the phone app stretched wide. The same Neon Dusk system, voice and rules, rebuilt for a big screen, a keyboard, a headset and an office.

Locked v1 · 7 Nov 2026. Approved by the founder. Desktop browser ships first (beta), then the mobile browser, then the Android app. Changes from here go into v2.

Who opens Bolo on a computer

The PRD names the first desktop user directly: Kamrul, who "practises in his car or private cabin", "values discretion above everything", and leaves "Web version timing … for the Kamrul persona (desktop at work)" as an open question. Three moments shape the desktop design.

Ten decisions

  1. A different layout, not a wide phoneThe phone's bottom tabs become a sidebar; swiped cards become two panes; sheets become centred dialogs. Content never stretches past 1120px.
  2. The scene goes cinematicOn a big screen the dusk is the reason to open the app. The talk fills the whole window with no sidebar, like a video call with no faces.
  3. Keyboard first, mouse alwaysSpace starts the talk and ends your turn, Esc ends, P pauses, R repeats, ↑↓ moves through feedback, 1–4 answers. Every key has a visible button too.
  4. One click before Bolo speaksBrowsers block sound until the user clicks. Desktop opens on "Meet Bolo"; that click unlocks audio and starts the welcome.
  5. Choose your microphoneComputers have several: laptop, headset, webcam. The mic check lists them, shows a live level, and remembers the choice.
  6. Headphones, said kindlyLaptop speakers feed Bolo's voice back into the mic. The mic check suggests headphones once, for clarity and for privacy.
  7. Discreet by defaultThe tab always says "Bolo", the tab icon has no face, nothing plays until Talk is pressed, and the talk pauses when you switch tabs.
  8. Feedback you can read side by sideStrength, three fixes and skills sit in a list on the left; the chosen card opens on the right. Your clip and the model clip sit next to each other.
  9. Reminders in the browser, asked for kindlyAfter the first talk, Bolo shows exactly what the notification will say, then the browser asks once. "Keep it private" hides what you talked about. The calendar and the phone stay as alternatives.
  10. Same account, same BoloTalks, feedback, Day 1 clip and plan follow you between phone and computer. Plans compare side by side; bKash shows a QR code to scan.

Every interactive element has a hover state and the 2px focus-ring outline, a pointer cursor, and a tooltip if it is icon-only. Nothing depends on hover or on a gesture: swiping becomes a list, long-press does not exist. Tab order follows the visual order; dialogs trap focus and return it on close.

Voice in a browser

  • Permission once, explained first. The consent card says why, then the browser asks for the mic. If it is blocked, Bolo shows where to unblock it in the address bar, with a picture of the browser's own icon.
  • Capture settings. Request the mic with echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain on. Record in Opus (WebM) on Chrome, Edge and Firefox, and AAC (MP4) on Safari; stream to the server in short chunks so scoring can start before the talk ends.
  • Autoplay. No sound before a click, ever. After "Meet Bolo" or "Talk", Bolo's voice and the ambient sound may play.
  • Tabs and sleep. When the tab is hidden, the talk pauses and the mic closes; coming back shows "Paused while you were away". A laptop lid closing is treated the same way.
  • Latency. Desktop users notice lag more, because they are sitting still. Keep the thinking arc rule (show it only after 600 ms) and target Bolo's reply within 1.5 s.

Discretion at work

  • The tab title is always Bolo, never "English practice" or the learner's goal; the tab icon is the faceless sun.
  • Nothing makes a sound until the learner presses a button; ambient sound is off on desktop until turned on.
  • Switching tabs pauses the talk and closes the mic.
  • Bolo never asks for notification permission when the page opens; it asks only after the first talk, from its own explanation card.
  • A notification shows "Bolo" and one third-person line. "Keep it private" turns that line into "Time for today's talk", never what you talked about.
  • The calendar event is titled "Talk with Bolo" with no other detail, so a shared calendar gives nothing away.

Build notes

  • Tech. The same Rive files for the companion and scenes (Rive has a web runtime); the scene needs a wide master. Web Audio for levels; MediaRecorder for capture.
  • Notifications. Web Push with a service worker. The server sends at most one a day, only if there was no talk that day and, on the free plan, only when free talks are left. On a computer they arrive only while the browser is running, which is why the calendar stays as a choice. On iPhone, web notifications need Bolo added to the Home Screen (iOS 16.4+).
  • Install. Make Bolo an installable web app (its own window and dock icon, no browser tabs), which also keeps it out of a shared browser history.
  • Performance. First screen interactive within 2.5 s on an office laptop; the scene as one animated canvas, not dozens of elements.
  • Accessibility. Keyboard-only and screen-reader runs (NVDA, VoiceOver) are part of every release, and the layout must reflow at 200% and 400% zoom.

Decided and still open

  • Decided (7 Nov): desktop is in the closed beta; the build order is desktop browser, then mobile browser, then the Android app; browser notifications are allowed.
  • Open: a "Rehearse a presentation" mode for desktop. Recommendation: not in the beta. Test demand first with a "Meeting rehearsal" scenario in the existing talk, and decide from how often testers pick it.
